Privacy without the fine print

Your camera roll never leaves this tab.

There is no upload button, no server-side conversion and no file storage. The first HEIC you add downloads a decoder once; every photo after that is processed on your own hardware. Close the tab and the session is gone.

JPEG output intentionally carries no camera details or GPS coordinates. The originals remain untouched on your device.

Good questions

The short
answers.

Everything else is in the HEIC to JPG guide.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. The converter is a web page that runs in your browser. Your files are read and converted on your own device and are never sent to a server. There is no upload endpoint.

Why does the JPG sometimes get bigger than the HEIC?

HEIC compresses photos more efficiently than JPG. The JPG trades file size for compatibility, which is the whole point: it opens on any computer, phone, website or printer. The contact sheet shows the real before and after size after every conversion.

Which quality should I choose?

Stay at 92 for photos you will keep or print. Choose 72–85 when an upload form has a strict size limit. Below about 70 you may see blocky edges in smooth areas such as skies.

Can I convert many photos at once?

Yes. Drop the whole selection. Each photo appears as its own print and you can download all of them in one zip. The practical limit is your device's memory.

What happens to Live Photos and metadata?

A Live Photo's still image becomes a JPG; the paired video is not included. Camera details, date taken and GPS location are not copied into the output because the JPG is drawn from decoded pixels.

Does it work offline?

Once the page and its HEIC decoder have loaded, conversion itself needs no network connection. Disconnect and try it — the files still develop.
